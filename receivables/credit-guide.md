# Customer credits

A customer credit is money a customer is owed back. It is a document on the Invoices list, of kind `customer_credit` (the amount a receipt applied to no invoice: a deposit before any invoice, or an overpayment) or `opening_credit` (from the conversion register). Both are settled the same way. All operations take `company_id`; run the dry check, show the review to the person and wait for approval before committing.

1. Create: `payment.record` with allocations that add up to less than the amount, or none. The setup needs `customer_credit_account_id` (an active liability account, set with `setup.configure`). The review shows `changes.kept_as_credit` with the amount and account; the committed receipt carries `credit.document_id`, and `payment.get` shows what is still `outstanding` on it. The entry debits the deposit account, credits each invoice's receivable control and credits the customer credit account.
2. Find: `credit.list` with `customer_id` reads that customer's credits with an amount left, oldest first, and their total. `credit.context` with `credit_document_id` (optional `search`, `after_id`) reads what is left on one credit, the same customer's unpaid invoices and the accounts a refund can be paid from. `document.list` with kind `customer_credit` reads every customer credit; `opening_credit` only those from the register.
3. Apply: `credit.apply` takes `credit_document_id`, `target_document_id` (an issued invoice of the same customer), `effective_date` and a positive decimal-string `amount`. The amount must fit what the credit has left and what the invoice still owes, on that date and every later recorded date. A receipt's credit and an invoice are on different accounts, so the application posts a transfer between them; an opening credit on the invoice's own control posts nothing (`posts` in the review).
4. Refund: `credit.refund` replaces the target with `cash_account_id`, an active asset account that is not a receivable control. It posts a debit to the credit's account and a credit to that account.
5. Reverse: `settlement.reverse` takes `settlement_id` and `effective_date` on or after the original. It appends a reversal, reverses the owned entry when there is one and gives the amount back to the credit and the invoice.

`reference` is optional on all three: supply your own unique one to make a retry safe (an identical retry returns the recorded settlement), or the settlement is named after its credit. `override_reason` is optional and recorded as an approval exception where one applies.

Applying a receipt's credit needs no second person: it is an allocation, as the receipt's own are. A refund, and every settlement of an opening credit, is checked against separation of duties: whoever recorded the receipt (or the opening register) cannot also settle it while the company requires separation, and the refusal says so.

`payment.reverse` removes a receipt's unused credit from the reversal date. If the credit has been applied or refunded, reverse those settlements first. Aging lists unused credits per customer, ageing from the credit's date, and nets them from what the customer owes; `control.tie_out` covers the customer credit account. All need `finance.receivables.payments.post` to write and receivables view to read; posting also needs ledger view and entries.post.
